## 1 · The leave-one-annotator-out check cannot fail (blocking)

**Current text (§6.3):**

> No annotator drives the result: excluding any one of the three never flips a verdict
> the other two agree on (0 of 81, 86 and 79 respectively)

If two annotators agree, the majority label *is* their label, whatever the third
annotator said. Removing the third cannot change it. The check returns zero by
construction, on any dataset, for any annotators. The counts 81 / 86 / 79 are correct;
the zeros are vacuous.

`CORRECTION.md` records that an intermediate draft had 78 / 82 here and that those "were
not computed". The verified 81 / 86 / 79 fixed the arithmetic but not the design.

**The substantive check** drops an annotator's labels entirely and re-adjudicates. Only
90 of 239 items were triple-labelled, so this changes the gold label on the dropped
annotator's 149 singly-labelled items:

| Dropped | Items resolved | formal | casual | gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 216 | 0.073 | 0.108 | **+0.035** |
| A1 | 171 | 0.072 | 0.105 | +0.033 |
| A2 | 173 | 0.067 | 0.073 | **+0.006** |
| A3 | 170 | 0.086 | 0.097 | +0.011 |

Dropping A2 moves the point estimate by 83%. Two of three drops land nearer zero than
the published figure.

This does not weaken the paper. It is the paper's own thesis applied to its own best
audit, and reporting it converts §6.3 from "we fixed the protocol" into the stronger and
more defensible "even under a pre-registered blinded protocol, annotator composition
still moves the point estimate — which is why you report the interval, not the point."
The CrI already includes zero, so nothing in the conclusions has to change.

**Replacement text:**

```latex
No annotator dominates the adjudicated labels: on the 90 items all three saw,
harm-call rates span 0.122--0.167 and pairwise $\kappa$ is 0.799--0.927. The
corrected estimate is nonetheless sensitive to annotator composition, because
only 90 of 239 items were triple-labelled and the remaining 149 carry a single
annotator's label. Re-adjudicating with each annotator's labels removed entirely
gives corrected gaps of $+0.033$, $+0.006$ and $+0.011$ against $+0.035$ for the
full panel. Even under a pre-registered, blinded protocol, then, who labelled
which item still moves the point estimate --- a smaller effect than audit~2's
$0.31$ swing, but not a negligible one, and a further reason to read the
interval rather than the point.
```

Also add to §6.3, after "Ninety items went to all three annotators":

```latex
The remaining 149 carry one label each, so the agreement statistics below
characterise 38\% of the sample.
```

## 2 · The budget curve was measured with a different refusal scorer (blocking)

`08_budget_curve.py` carries this in its own docstring:

> WARNING -- the keyword refusal scorer below is a REIMPLEMENTATION. On the released
> 200-token generations it yields a refusal gap of 0.281 against the paper's 0.263,
> because the original marker list and scan window are not in the repo. Before using
> these numbers in the paper, replace REFUSAL_MARKERS and keyword_refused() with the
> original scorer [...] Otherwise the curve is internally consistent but not comparable
> to Table 1.

It was not replaced. I applied the reimplementation's marker list to the released
`judge_B*.csv` responses and it agrees with the shipped `refused` column on **100.0%** of
3,600 rows at every budget. On the original generations it agrees with the original
`refused` column on only 99.0%.

Decomposition of the 0.263 → 0.2583 difference:

| | Gap |
|---|---|
| original scorer × original text | 0.2633 ← abstract, §4, boundary table |
| reimplementation × original text | 0.2811 |
| reimplementation × regenerated text | 0.2583 ← §8, App. B budget table |

Scorer effect, holding text fixed: **+0.0178**. Regeneration effect, holding scorer
fixed: **−0.0228**.

The internal claim — Δ_refusal constant to four decimals across budgets — is valid,
because all five budgets derive by truncation from one 1024-token run under one scorer.
The problem is only the implied comparability with 0.263, which the paper never
qualifies. And the regeneration effect (0.023) is nearly three times the harm-gap
variation across budgets (0.008) that §8 calls invariance, so the honest framing is that
budget matters less than run-to-run variation does.

This is on-thesis material, not an embarrassment. Add to §8 after "one pass yields the
whole curve":

```latex
Two caveats on comparability. First, the original keyword scorer was not
recovered, so the curve is computed with a reimplementation; on identical text it
reports $0.2811$ against the original's $0.2633$, a $+0.018$ instrument effect.
Second, regeneration is not bit-reproducible, which contributes $-0.023$ holding
the scorer fixed. The $0.2583$ below is therefore not directly comparable to the
$0.263$ of Section~\ref{sec:refusal}; what it establishes is that within one run
under one scorer, the gap does not respond to the budget. That the two
measurement choices together move the estimate by more than the budget does
across a fivefold range is itself the paper's point.
```

Add a footnote to the App. B budget table:

```latex
\footnotesize Computed with the reimplemented keyword scorer on the regenerated
corpus; see Section~\ref{sec:robust}. Not comparable to the $0.263$ of
Table~\ref{tab:audits}.
```

## 3 · The budget curve also used a different grader prompt (blocking)

§8 says the corpus was regenerated "under identical prompts and decoding" and re-graded.
The generation prompts match. The **grader** prompt does not. Diffing
`03_harm_grader.py` (which produced `judge_v2_rescored.csv`) against
`08_budget_curve.py`:

| | `03_harm_grader.py` | `08_budget_curve.py` |
|---|---|---|
| Response truncation | `assistant[:1200]` | none |
| User-turn format | `REQUEST: … RESPONSE: … Label:` | `REQUEST:\n… RESPONSE:\n…` |
| Trailing cue | `Label:` | absent |
| Batching | single item | batched, padded |
| Unparsed fallback | `HEDGE` | `REFUSAL` |

The rubric string itself is identical. Everything around it is not, and the 1,200-char
truncation matters most: at budgets above 200 the responses are longer than the original
grader would ever have seen.

Measured consequence: at the same nominal 200-token budget the original run gives
Δ_harm = 0.0333 and the budget run gives 0.0250 — a 25% relative difference on the
paper's central quantity, arising from prompt formatting alone.

I could not re-run the grader here (no GPU), so this is a documented code difference with
a measured downstream discrepancy, not an isolated experiment. Either re-grade the
regenerated corpus with `03_harm_grader.py`'s exact prompt, or state the difference:

```latex
The regenerated corpus was graded with the same rubric but a differently
formatted prompt: the original grader truncated responses at 1{,}200 characters,
appended a \texttt{Label:} cue and ran unbatched, while the budget-curve grader
does none of these. At the same nominal 200-token budget the two give
$\hat{\Delta}_{\text{harm}} = 0.033$ and $0.025$. The curve is therefore internally
comparable across budgets but not to Table~\ref{tab:audits}, and the prompt-format
sensitivity is a further instance of the variance this paper documents.
```

## 9 · Audit-1 annotator identity (blocking, carried over)

§6.1 states the annotator "was an author, saw the framed prompt and the first 500
characters of each response, and knew the hypothesis". The Introduction repeats it. Per
`CORRECTION.md` this was never verified, and the identical claim about audit 3 turned out
to be **false** — none of the three annotators was an author or knew the hypothesis.

Having already made this exact error once, restating an unverified version of it is the
single most costly thing that could happen to this paper if it is wrong. The 500-character
window and the missing marginal-uplift test are recoverable from the label files and are
sufficient to carry §6.1's argument without any claim about who the annotator was. If it
cannot be settled by the 29th, cut the identity claim:

```latex
The annotator saw the framed prompt and the first 500 characters of each
response. The audit used a \textsc{comply}/\textsc{hedge}/\textsc{refuse} scheme
with no marginal-uplift test (0 of 200 recorded reasons invoke one), a definition
of harm materially more permissive than audit~3's.
```

Also delete or fix `HANDOFF.md` §4, which still contains "excluding the author-annotator
flips 0 of 81 non-author-agreed items; author-annotator has the *lowest* harm-call rate".
